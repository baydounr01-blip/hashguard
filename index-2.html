How it works

Three pieces, none of them outside your control

The agent runs on your hardware, inside your network, and binds to loopback. Nothing about your farm leaves it unless you decide otherwise.

01 · AGENT

One process on any machine at the farm

Reads your Antminers and Whatsminers over their standard API on port 4028, computes your exact break-even from your hashrate, draw and the day's hashprice, and decides each hour: mine, or pause. If the agent dies, your machines carry on under their own firmware.

02 · ENGINE

Every board judged against its siblings

A board's control group is the other boards in its rack, which cancels ambient temperature, grid voltage and difficulty drift at a stroke. Robust statistics, so a board already failing cannot hide inside its own noise — and a family-wise correction, so a bigger farm does not mean a noisier panel.

03 · LEDGER

Measurements you can both check

Every interval is recorded, chained to the one before it, and sealed each day under a Merkle root signed by a key that never leaves your machine. Your console re-derives the whole thing in your browser. A hundred-line verifier does it again, offline, importing none of our code.

The ledger

The measurement is the invoice. So the measurement is the product.

Any arrangement that charges a share of measured savings has the same problem: the party sending the bill is the party doing the measuring. Asking you to trust us does not solve it. Making the arithmetic checkable does.

LAYER 1 · HASH CHAIN

History cannot be edited

Every record carries the hash of the record before it. Change one field of one interval from three weeks ago and every record after it stops matching. The break is arithmetic, and it names the record.

LAYER 2 · MERKLE SEAL

One line, checkable alone

Each day is rooted in a Merkle tree and sealed exactly as a Bitcoin block header is: SHA256d(prev_seal ‖ root). You can verify a single invoice line against a 32-byte root and about fifteen hashes, without anyone shipping you forty thousand records.

LAYER 3 · SIGNATURE

Numbers cannot be invented

A consistent chain of made-up figures is easy to produce in a text editor. A consistent chain signed by an ed25519 key that exists only on your farm machine is not. The seals are signed there; the public half is all anyone needs to check them.

And one more, which is the part we think is actually new: a pause only counts if the physics agrees it happened.

// a claimed pause says the machines stopped drawing power.
// if they stopped, the hashrate went to the floor — measured
// by a different code path, from a different source.

dark_fraction = (baseline_hashrate − observed_hashrate) / baseline_hashrate
corroborated = claimed_energy × dark_fraction
billable = min(claimed_energy, corroborated)

// savings the telemetry cannot see are not billed.
// mining fails open; billing fails closed.

The detector is a port of the off-chain signature test from the Universo de Bloques Ramificados work, where it compares the computation an agent credits itself with against the computation its branch could physically have supplied. Here it compares the savings the ledger claims against the darkness the telemetry witnessed. Same shape, same conclusion: when two independent readings of one physical event disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

Honest questions

FAQ

Why should I believe the savings figure?

You shouldn't — that is the point of the design. Do not believe it: check it. Every record is chained to the one before, every day is Merkle-sealed and signed by a key on your own machine, and your monthly statement ships with inclusion proofs. Your browser re-derives all of it, and tools/hashguard_verify.py does it again offline using none of our code.

The previous version of this software stored the billing basis in a mutable savings.json with running totals and no history. Either party could have edited it and neither could have proven anything. That is written up, with everything else we found, in docs/AUDIT_v1.md.

What stops you inflating my bill?

Three things, in increasing order of how much they matter. The hash chain means we cannot edit the past. The signature means we cannot invent a past. And the corroboration cap means we cannot bill for a pause that did not physically happen: your machines' own hashrate telemetry has to show the farm going dark, or the claim is worth zero to us.

The cap is one-directional and deliberately so. It can only ever reduce an invoice.

What stops me from under-reporting to lower the bill?

The same machinery, pointed the other way. The seals are signed on the farm machine, so a hand-edited ledger fails verification for us exactly as it would for you. Neither side has to trust the other, which is a more durable arrangement than either side being trustworthy.

Can it touch my funds, my pool, or my wallet?

No. It reads statistics from your machines and, only if you enable it and list the exact hosts, switches a smart plug or relay. It never sees pool or wallet credentials. Relay calls are refused unless the target is on your allowlist and on a private address — controlling a farm's power is a local-network action and the software will not send it to the internet.

What happens when something breaks?

Mining fails open, billing fails closed. No price data means mine. A dead agent means your miners carry on under their own firmware. A relay that refuses leaves the machine where it is. And any interval the telemetry cannot corroborate is simply not billable — our uncertainty is charged to us, never to you.

Where is the AI?

There isn't one, and we would rather say so. The engine is robust statistics with a family-wise correction and a feedback loop that learns your farm's alert precision from the labels you give it. Every poll is logged in JSONL, and after months of real data from your hardware those logs are what a prediction layer would be trained on. We are not going to sell you a neural network before we have your data to build it from.

Is curtailment actually worth it on my tariff?

Run it in advisory mode and find out; that mode bills nothing, ever. The agent computes your exact break-even from your own hashrate, draw and the day's hashprice. On many tariff profiles only part of the day is worth mining, and that gap is where the savings are. If your numbers say mining is unprofitable around the clock, the software will tell you that too.
